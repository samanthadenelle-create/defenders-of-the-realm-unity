## 0. The one idea — why the game is called *Echoes of Elarion*

Elarion was once the great heart of the realm — its tree, its city, its commerce, its song. The age
of Elarion ended. What is left is not the thing itself but its **echoes**: the ruined halls the Folk
once filled, the roads that now fray into the wild, the **crystal** that still seeps from where the
Heart's power ran deepest, and the Hollow Ones — people unmade, *echoing* the lives they can no longer
live. An echo is a sound after the source is gone. The whole world is the sound the realm is still
making, a hundred winters after it should have fallen silent.

The player is the **Keeper** — the one who can still hear the source under the echo, and who chooses
to answer it: to **rebuild** Elarion from its rubble, **defend** its Heart against the rot that wants
the last of the song, and walk out into the four ruined marches to reclaim what the greater age left
behind. *Echoes of Elarion* is the story of holding a note long after the singer is gone — and slowly,
stone by stone, teaching the realm to sing again.

> **Brand alignment:** This is exactly the BRAND_BIBLE premise — *"Elarion was the realm's great center
> of commerce, now decimated to ruin; the player rebuilds it from the rubble while defending the rising
> settlement."* The "Echoes" frame names *why* there is rubble to rebuild and crystal to harvest: they
> are the remnant of a greater age. The subtitle **"Alerion's Awakening"** is the long shadow over it —
> the apex power (the dragon **Alerion**) stirring as the realm wakes. (See §7.)

## 1. The Premise — The Fall and the Echoes

### The greater age
Before memory, the world cracked open in its deep places and left a **Wound**. To answer it, the
**First Light** planted a tree in a green valley and named it **Elarion** — the Heart-Grove. Crystal
grew in its bark; magic rose through its roots; and around it the Folk built not a hamlet but a
**realm** — Elarion the city, the realm's great center of commerce, its roads running out to a dozen
valleys, its markets loud, its wards strong, its reach long. This was the greater age. It lasted a
thousand years.

### The Fall
From the Wound seeps the **Withering** — a slow cold rot that takes from living things until they are
hollow, hungry, and remembering only what they used to be. For a thousand years the Heart held it
back. Then the Withering crept close, and the realm could not hold its full reach. The far valleys
fell, one by one, in a line that points — when you draw it — straight back at the Wound. The roads
closed. The markets emptied. The wards along the marches began to go dark. Elarion, the great city,
**decimated to ruin** — its outer halls abandoned, its people drawn back inside the wall to the one
thing still worth defending: the Heart at its center.

### The Echoes
The greater age did not vanish cleanly. It left **echoes**:
- **Ruins** — watchtowers, crofts, ward-circles, drowned halls. The Folk built *further out than the
  Keeper can now hold*; the rot took the rest. Every ruin is the realm's old reach, gone quiet.
- **Crystal** — where the Heart's power ran, it left seams of crystal in the stone. The deeper and more
  dangerous the place, the stronger and stranger the crystal: the echo of the Heart's song, frozen in
  rock. (See §6 and the four regions, §3–5.)
- **The Hollow Ones** — people the Withering unmade. They are the saddest echo of all: a person after
  the person is gone, still marching, still wearing the shape of a life. *They are grief, not villains.*

### Why the Heart is defended, and why the hordes come
The Heart is the only barrier between the realm the Folk remember and the cold, silent one closing in.
If it falls, the Withering does not stop at the valley — it does not stop anywhere. The Hollow Ones
march on the Heart **not out of hate** but because the Heart is the only thing that can end what they
have become, and the only way they know to end it is to break it. They want the silence. Every wave is
the dark testing whether the Keeper will hold the note as long as the greater age did.

### Why the player builds (the loop, in fiction)
The Keeper does not inherit a city. The Keeper inherits a *ruin with a heartbeat*. So the core verb is
**rebuild**: clear the rubble, raise the walls, place the towers, claim the crystal seams, and push the
realm's reach back out march by march — exactly the CoC×Warcraft loop of NORTH_STAR. The fiction *is*
the mechanics:

| Fiction | Loop verb (NORTH_STAR) |
|---|---|
| A ruined city you raise from rubble | **BUILD** (walls, towers, structures — placement=role) |
| Crystal seams left by the Heart's old power | **HARVEST** (resource nodes; danger gates grade — §6, WO-144) |
| The dark testing the note | **DEFEND** (the wave/raid loop) |
| The realm slowly waking while you sleep | **OFFLINE** (mines + pets accrue) |
| The apex power stirring as the realm wakes | **Alerion's Awakening** (the apex dragon escalation — §7) |

## 2. The Heart of Elarion

The **Heart of Elarion** is the world-tree / stone reliquary at the center of the village (scene centre,
the thing the whole game defends — `CLAUDE.md` §7). It is an ancient, sentient thing, ten thousand
summers old, planted at the realm's founding by the First Light. Its bark is veined with crystal; its
roots descend into the wellsprings of the world's magic; and it **sings** — a held note only those bound
to it can hear. The Keeper hears it the way you hear a held breath.

- **What it does:** holds back the Withering by its presence (within the valley, things grow; beyond it,
  things forget themselves), feeds the village with light and the slow drip of crystal, and speaks — but
  only to its Keeper, in the song.
- **How it shows feeling:** the crystal in its canopy is its expression — steady violet when the realm is
  well, ambering to red to a heavy purple-red as threat mounts; the tree itself answers more slowly, leaves
  yellowing at half-wound, browning and falling at deep wound.
- **Why it matters:** it is not "important." It is everything. While the Heart sings, the realm has a
  reason to be rebuilt; if it falls, there is nothing to rebuild toward.

> **Naming note (important — see Appendix A):** Canon is the **Heart of Elarion = the world-tree / stone
> reliquary** (`CLAUDE.md` §7; `docs/narrative-bible.md`). A separate doc, `docs/STORYLINE.md`, reframes
> the Heart as a *burned tree replaced by a Cathedral Spire*. That reframe is **not** adopted as canon
> here — it contradicts `CLAUDE.md` §7 and the rebrand premise (you rebuild *Elarion*, the Heart still
> sings). This bible keeps the living-Heart canon. The Spire reframe is flagged in Appendix A as a
> reconciliation item for the owner, not silently merged.

## 3–5. The Four Regions — danger out, reward out, story all the way

The realm's old reach survives as **four marches**, one out of each cardinal gate, dressed by WO-142 and
graded by WO-144. The through-line is the bible's dial — **warmth in, dread out**. Closest to the Heart,
the echoes are gentle: working farms, lit lanterns, old roads still used. Farthest out, the echoes are
violent: ruined wards, drowned halls, the rot still spreading. **And here is the load-bearing lore: the
echoes are strongest, strangest, and most volatile where the Fall was worst.** The crystal — the Heart's
power frozen in stone — concentrates and *darkens* toward the places the Withering hit hardest. So the
danger of a march and the richness of its crystal are *the same fact, told twice*: you cannot have one
without the other. **Danger gates reward because the reward is made of danger.**

| March | Dir | Dread (WO-142) | Crystal grade (WO-144) | The echo it holds |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Goldfields** | E | low | Aether / Verdant | The realm's old breadbasket, still peopled — the last open road |
| **Stoneback Ridge** | W | neutral | Verdant | The realm's old quarry and the bones older than Elarion itself |
| **Mirewood** | S | heavy | Mire | The first valley the Fall ever took, drowned and remembering |
| **Corrupted Ashwood** | N | front | Wraith | The front line — the realm's old wards, failing, nearest the Wound |

### 3. East — The Goldfields *(common Aether, fading to Verdant)*

**The echo it holds:** the realm's old breadbasket — the warmest, most peopled march, where Elarion's
commerce still faintly breathes. Carts still come. Wheat still grows, because the fields lie in the
Heart's lee. But the browning edge creeps a little closer each season; the road closes mile by mile.
The Goldfields are the echo of the realm *at its best* — the open road, the loud market — heard faint
but heard.

**Why its crystal is common (Aether/Verdant):** the Fall barely touched the Goldfields. The Heart's
power here is *steady, near, and gentle* — so the crystal it left is the **baseline grade, Aether**,
the bread-and-butter the whole economy runs on, with the first step up to **Verdant** (the "living
lands" grade) at the field's far edge where the song begins to thin. Common crystal is the reward of
safe ground: plentiful, low-risk, the tutorial harvest. The danger is low; so is the grade. *This is the
correlation at its gentlest.*

**The people of the echo:** *Maeren the Roadwarden* keeps the eastern road open out of stubbornness;
*Brightwheat* the field-elder sows a little closer to the wall each year and grieves the rows he has
lost; *Sister Wren* the wandering almsgiver carries news of which valleys still stand. (Full NPC lore:
`docs/regions-narrative-and-npcs.md` §2.)

> **Voice — region intro:** *"The east road still opens. Carts still come, fewer each season. This is
> the last of the realm that remembers being loud. Light a stone further out, Keeper, and it remembers
> a little longer."*

### 4. West — Stoneback Ridge *(Verdant, from bones older than the realm)*

**The echo it holds:** the realm's old quarry — and an echo older than the realm itself. High cold stone
above the valley, where frost-spirits dwelled before the First Light ever planted the Heart. Elarion cut
its finest crystal from these high seams once; the workings are abandoned now, the cairns and standing
stones left to the wind. The ridge does not care who wins. It is the echo of *deep time* — the realm was
a guest here, and the stone outlasts guests.

**Why its crystal is Verdant (neutral danger, mid reward):** the Heart's roots reach deep under the ridge,
so the seams are **rich** — the deepest crystal in the realm runs here. But the Fall passed the ridge by;
the danger is *neutral*, not heavy. So the grade tops out at **Verdant** — a step above the safe farm, the
mid-game prize — gated less by the rot and more by the *climb*: steep terrain, fewer but tougher enemies,
ground that slows everyone. The ridge's reward is paid in effort, not in dread. *The one march where the
correlation bends: hard to reach, but not hostile.*

**The people of the echo:** *Garrick the Last Hewer*, the last of a quarry crew that cut crystal from the
high seams and stayed when the others left; and *the Elder Cold (the Frostmother)*, an ancient frost-spirit
older than Elarion, who measures the Keeper against ten thousand winters. (Full lore:
`docs/regions-narrative-and-npcs.md` §3.)

> **Voice — region intro:** *"The ridge was old before the Heart was a seed. It cut us our finest crystal
> and asked nothing. It still asks nothing. Climb careful, Keeper — the cold is patient, and it is older
> than patience."*

### 5. South — Mirewood *(Mire grade — the first valley the Fall took)*

**The echo it holds:** the **first valley the Withering ever took**, drowned now under black water. Its
hall and homes still stand beneath the murk — a whole settlement of the greater age, sunk and remembering.
The main tide of Hollow Ones marches up from here, because the Withering pools in still water the way cold
pools in a cellar. The fog never lifts; the footing lies. Mirewood is the echo of the *first death* — the
realm learning, for the first time, that it could lose.

**Why its crystal is Mire grade (heavy danger, juicy reward):** the Fall *began* here, so the Heart's old
power lies thick and curdled in the drowned stone — crystal that grew rich in a place soaked in the
Withering's first work. The grade is **Mire**: rarer, more valuable, and **only harvestable here**, under
the heaviest enemy pressure in the realm. You cannot farm it safe; you must *hold a forward position in the
murk* to drain it. The danger is heavy; the reward steps up to match. *This is "danger gates reward" made
literal — the richest common-route crystal sits in the most dangerous reachable ground.*

**The people of the echo:** *Old Sedge the Ferryman*, a silent boatman who poles the black water for a
small strange price; and *Vessa the Lantern-Widow*, the last living soul of a drowned hamlet, keeping one
lantern lit for folk who will not return — and holding the drowned hall's record of the first Fall. (Full
lore: `docs/regions-narrative-and-npcs.md` §4.)

> **Voice — region intro:** *"The water keeps everything it takes. There is a town down there, Keeper —
> the first one we lost. The crystal it leaves is rich and wrong, and the only way to it is through the
> tide. Hold the dry ground. Drain it fast."*

### 5b. North — Corrupted Ashwood *(Wraith grade — the front line, nearest the Wound)*

**The echo it holds:** the realm's old wards, failing. The dead forest — ash-grey ground, trees that died
standing, violet corruption-fog between the trunks — is the closest the living realm reaches toward the
**Wound**, the source of the Withering. The first Keepers planted their *strongest* ward-stones along the
north march to hold this line; they are going dark now, one by one, and each that fails lets the Ashwood
creep closer. Ashwood is the echo of the realm's *last defense* — and it is still happening. The ruins here
are not ancient; they are **recent**, the rot still spreading through them.

**Why its crystal is Wraith grade (the front line, the rarest and richest):** this is where the Fall is
*worst and ongoing*, so the Heart's power here is the most corrupted, most concentrated, most volatile — the
crystal turned by the Withering into something powerful and faintly *wrong*. **Wraith** is the rarest,
richest grade in the realm, and it drops only in Ashwood, on ground at the very edge of the rot. To hold a
Wraith node is to fortify a position on the front line and drain it before the dark takes it back. This
crystal pays for the *apex* of the build ladder — the pieces nothing else can buy. **The most dangerous
harvest yields the best reward, because the echo is strongest where the Fall is worst. That is the whole
economy's story, told in one march.**

**The people of the echo:** *Old Bram*, the last north-warden, half-Withered, holding one stone alight by
will alone; and *the One Who Remembers* — a Hollow One that comes to the edge of a lit ward, does not raise
a weapon, tilts its head, and seems to know the Keeper's name. It is the bible's whole thesis in one figure:
grief wearing armor. (Full lore: `docs/regions-narrative-and-npcs.md` §5.)

> **Voice — region intro:** *"North is the Wound's own breath. Our strongest wards held here, and they are
> failing. The crystal is rich and it is wrong — it is the song gone to rot. Take what you can. Do not stay
> past dark. Do not linger where the song is thin, Keeper. The north forgets people who stand still."*

## 6. The Echoes — ruins and crystal as remnants of the greater age

The "Echoes" theme has two physical forms in the world. Both say the same thing: *a greater age was here,
and this is the sound it is still making.*

### The ruins (WO-142)
Ruins are the strongest single device for "lived-in and ancient." They are the echoes of the realm's old
**reach** — proof the Folk built further out than the Keeper can now hold. There is no pristine "ruins"
style; they are *composed from broken pieces* (toppled walls, rubble, half-sunk crofts, fallen watchtowers,
broken ward-circles), which is exactly right: a ruin is a whole thing remembered in fragments. Read the
warmth-in/dread-out dial across them: Goldfields has the *least* ruin (an old roadside shrine-stone, a
tumbled fence — age, not collapse); Ashwood has the *most* (a fallen watchtower, a broken ward-circle with
one stone still faintly violet, rubble everywhere — collapse that is still happening). A broken ward-circle
sitting beside a march's ward-stone is the realm's old defense and the Keeper's new one, side by side:
**relight the ward, and the broken circle is, for a moment, whole again.** That is the Echoes theme in a
single image — the past completed by the present.

### The crystal (WO-144)
Crystal is the echo of the **Heart's song frozen in stone** — and its grade is a map of the Fall:
- **Aether** (common, anywhere) — the steady baseline, the Heart's gentle near-song. The bread-and-butter.
- **Verdant** (Goldfields/Stoneback) — the song of the living lands, a step richer; the safe-march reward.
- **Mire** (Mirewood) — the song soaked in the first Fall: curdled, rich, only won under heavy pressure.
- **Wraith** (Ashwood) — the song gone to rot at the front line: rarest, richest, faintly wrong; won only
  on the most dangerous ground.

The lore *is* the economy: **the rarer the crystal, the worse the Fall that made it, the more dangerous the
ground that holds it.** A player walking into Ashwood for Wraith crystal is, in fiction, reaching into the
deepest echo of the realm's death to pull out the thing that will rebuild it. There is **no in-economy
exchange** — you cannot trade safe Aether up to Wraith — because you cannot fake having stood on the front
line. Danger gates reward, and the gate is the story.

> **Why this matters for the live game (NORTH_STAR tie):** the danger=reward crystal ladder is the harvest
> spine of the CoC×Warcraft loop and the high end of the premium/Pi sink. The lore makes the grind *mean*
> something: every Wraith crystal is a sentence of the realm's history. That is the difference between a
> number going up and a story being told while the number goes up.

## 7. Alerion's Awakening — the apex over it all

The brand's subtitle is **"Alerion's Awakening."** Per BRAND_BIBLE, **Alerion** is the *apex that awakens*
— a heraldic eagle-form mapped onto the game's apex dragon, the one-letter kin of *Elarion* (realm vs. the
power that named it). The narrative frame: as the Keeper rebuilds the realm and its song grows louder, the
oldest power in the world — the apex that the realm was *named for* — begins to stir. The greater the echo
the Keeper raises, the more it wakes. Rebuilding Elarion is not only restoration; it is an *alarm*. The
apex dragon is the title event and the ceiling of the escalation: the thing that comes when the realm has
woken enough to be worth answering.

> **Reconciliation flag (Appendix A):** an existing antagonist thread names the apex flyer **"Syndrath the
> Devourer"** (`docs/STORYLINE.md` §9) and the necromancer **"Alduin the Mournful"** (`narrative-bible.md`).
> The rebrand introduces **Alerion** as the apex/title power. These need owner reconciliation — they may be
> the same being under a rebranded name, or distinct (Alerion the apex dragon; Alduin the necromancer-march
> leader). This bible treats **Alerion** as the brand-level apex and leaves Alduin as the march antagonist,
> but does not silently rename code/canon identifiers (`DragonBoss`, `Alduin`). See Appendix A.

## 8. Tone & Voice Guide (for all in-game text)

Inherits `docs/narrative-bible.md` §8 wholesale; the additions below are for the *Echoes/rebuild/region*
surfaces the rebrand adds.

### The core voice
- **Short sentences with weight.** Grounded vocabulary. Slightly old bones — modern English, no "thee/thou,"
  no "Hark!"
- **Hopeful, never grimdark.** This is *ruin you restore*, not ruin you wallow in (BRAND_BIBLE §4). Warm
  dusk palette. The Fall is real, but the through-line is rebuilding.
- **The song** is the recurring metaphor for the Heart's voice and the realm's life. Crystal is "the song
  frozen / the song in the stone." Ruins are "the realm's old reach." Echoes are everywhere — use the word
  sparingly so it keeps its weight.
- **Quiet stakes, concrete details.** Bread on the workbench, a candle by the door, a tail twitching in
  sleep. Specifics over speeches.

### Three things the Echoes frame adds
1. **The past is present tense.** Ruins and crystal aren't backstory the player reads — they're *here, now,
   being walked through*. Write region/ruin barks as the world *still happening*: "Someone lived here.
   Someone is still here, faintly." Not "long ago, a great city stood."
2. **Warmth in, dread out — in the sentence length.** Goldfields lines are longer, warmer, conversational.
   Ashwood lines are short, clipped, fraying — the prose itself should thin as the player walks north.
3. **Rebuild is the verb.** Building/placement text is *restoration*, not construction: "raise," "relight,"
   "reclaim," "the Folk built this long before you," "where one tower sings, the dark slows." The Keeper is
   answering an echo, not starting from nothing.

### Surface-by-surface
- **Region intros** (first time the Keeper steps through a gate): one short paragraph in that march's
  register — see the four `> Voice` blocks in §3–5. Warm and inviting east; clipped and wrong north.
- **NPC barks** (proximity speech, `AmbientNPC` bubble): in-character, present-tense, one idea per bark.
  Use the canon NPC voices from `docs/regions-narrative-and-npcs.md` §2–5 verbatim where they exist.
- **Building/structure descriptions:** restoration framing (above). Reuse `narrative-bible.md` §7.13 for the
  five named buildings; for new placeable pieces, write to "the Folk built these long before you" — the
  catalog is the realm's *remembered* vocabulary, not invented.
- **Crystal/harvest flavor:** tie grade to place and danger (§6). Aether = "the Heart's slow, steady gift."
  Wraith = "the song gone to rot — rich, and wrong, and only here." Never explain the economy in numbers in
  flavor text; let the *feel* carry the risk/reward.
- **Ruin markers** (future lore hooks): one line that says *someone was here and the realm was bigger* —
  e.g. "The Folk raised a watchtower here once. It looked north so the village wouldn't have to."
- **Wave/Heart-state lines:** keep `narrative-bible.md` §7.3–7.7 — they're canon-locked and on-tone.

### Don'ts
- No fake archaic English, no grimdark nihilism, no heroes-vs-villains framing (the Hollow Ones are *grief*,
  not Sauron), no exposition dumps, no joke breaks. Humor comes from the pets' cuteness, never the prose.
- Don't overuse "echo/echoes" in player-facing copy — it's the *theme*, not a tic. Let the world *be* an
  echo; rarely *say* it.
