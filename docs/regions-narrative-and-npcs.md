## 0. The one rule that makes exploration work — the ward-tether

The bible is firm: **the Keeper cannot leave the Heart for long without losing the bond.** So how does a Defend-the-Town game become Defend-**and-Explore** (Rung 3) without breaking that?

The answer is already in canon — the **ward-stones** ("raised by the first Keepers… they answer your call"). Reframe them as the leash that lets the Keeper roam:

> The Heart's song reaches only so far past the walls. The first Keepers planted **ward-stones** along the four marches — each one carries the song a little further out. A lit ward-stone is a place the Keeper can stand and still hear home. **Relight a march's ward-stone, and you can walk that far.**

This single idea unifies everything:

- **Exploration is gated and earned** — you push into each region by relighting its ward-stones, one at a time. Distance unlocks like a ladder, not a flag.
- **Resource nodes have a reason** — claiming a crystal seam / gold field / cold-iron vein means raising a ward beside it, which both extends your reach *and* starts the node harvesting (the WO-110/111 hook).
- **It explains the danger curve** — the further from a lit ward, the thinner the song, the stronger the Withering, the harder the enemies. Tonal dread scales with distance, exactly as the bible says ("beyond the valley, things forget themselves").
- **It ties the Arcane Tower in** — the ward-stones *are* the Arcane Tower's lore. Building/relighting wards in the field is the same magic as raising the ward-spire at home.

Each region below is structured the same way: a **premise**, a **plotline** (the thread the Keeper follows as they relight that march), the **NPCs** who live or linger there, and **what you claim**.

## 1. The tonal gradient — warmth in, dread out

Distance from the Heart is the dial. Use it to keep both the cozy core and the Souls-like ambition true at once:

| Region | Direction | Feeling | Where it sits on the dial |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Goldfields** | East | Warm, pastoral, the last open road | Closest to hope — other valleys still stand out here |
| **Stoneback Ridge** | West | High, cold, old, indifferent | Neutral — the mountain does not care who wins |
| **Mirewood** | South | Drowned, oppressive, watchful | Heavy — the first valley the Withering took lies under the water |
| **Corrupted Ashwood** | North | Dead, hushed, wrong | The front line — you are walking toward the Wound |

The village (Elarion) stays the warm hearth at the center. The regions are where the range lives.

## 2. East — The Goldfields · *"The Long Road"*

### Premise
The eastern fields still grow, because they lie in the Heart's lee. This is the last open road — wanderers, almsfolk, and the carts of valleys still standing pass through here, carrying news and grain and rumor. But the Withering is patient: the wheat browns a little further from the village each season. The Goldfields are where the player meets the wider world the bible only hints at ("brought by wanderers from valleys still standing"), and where they first understand the war is bigger than one valley.

### Plotline — *The Long Road*
A caravan limps in from the east with fewer carts than it left with. The road is closing, mile by mile, as roaming Hollow Ones cut the open ground between valleys. The Keeper relights the eastern ward-stones to push the song back out over the road — reopening trade, sheltering the folk who arrive, and learning that the eastern valleys are falling one by one in a line that points, when you draw it, straight back toward the Wound. The thread ends with a choice: hold the road open as a lifeline (more wanderers, more trade, more mouths to defend) or let it close and pull everyone inside the walls.

### NPCs

**Maeren the Roadwarden** — old caravan-guard, grey at the temples, keeps the eastern road open out of stubbornness more than hope. Practical, weary, kind under the gruffness. The player's guide to the Goldfields and the first to explain the ward-tether in plain terms.
> *"Roads don't close because the dark says so. They close when the last cart stops coming. Mine hasn't yet. Light me a stone further out and it won't today either."*

**Brightwheat** — field-elder who has worked the eastern acres his whole life and remembers when the harvest reached the horizon. He watches the browning edge creep in and counts the rows he's lost. Gives the player the gold/grain resource node and the ache of slow loss.
> *"My grandfather sowed to the far ridge. My father to the elms. I sow to the fencepost now. I would like to sow further before I'm done."*

**Sister Wren** — a wandering almsgiver and rough healer from a valley that fell two winters ago. Carries news of which valleys still stand and which have gone quiet. She knew healers who walked toward the Wound to seal it — a quiet thread to Alduin's origin, never stated outright.
> *"I have buried three valleys' worth of kind people. I keep walking east to west because the walking is the only prayer I have left."*

### What you claim
The **Goldfields grain-and-gold node** — open, visible, easy to reach but hard to hold (enemies cross the open ground in formation, seen from far off). The tutorial node for the harvest loop.

## 3. West — Stoneback Ridge · *"The Cold-Wandered's Kin"*

### Premise
High cold stone above the valley. Frost-spirits older than Elarion itself dwell in the peaks — they were here before the First Light planted the tree and they are indifferent to its war, which makes them more unsettling than any enemy. The deepest crystal seams run under the ridge, where the Heart's roots reach. And the ridge holds a private grief: the **Ice Wolf came down from here the night the previous Keeper fell**, and the old Keeper's trail led west before it turned north into the dark.

### Plotline — *The Cold-Wandered's Kin*
The frost-spirits are stirring — not attacking, but *watching*, which is worse. The Keeper climbs the ridge relighting wards as they go and finds the high places colder than they should be, the cold leaking down toward the valley. Following the Ice Wolf's old path, they find a sign of the previous Keeper's passage (a dropped focus, a ward half-lit and abandoned mid-ritual) and learn the master came this way before the end — and that the frost-spirits *let them pass*. The thread resolves at the Elder Cold, who will decide whether the mountain hardens against the Keeper or lends its winter to the war. Befriend it and the ridge becomes an ally; rouse it and the west becomes the hardest march.

### NPCs

**The Elder Cold** *(the Frostmother)* — an ancient frost-spirit, the Ice Wolf's elder and something like its mother. Vast, slow, speaks in long pauses, measures the Keeper against ten thousand winters and is not easily impressed. Not hostile, not kind — *old*. The Ice Wolf's loyalty is the only thing that earns the Keeper a hearing.
> *"…The little wolf went down the mountain. …It has not come back to tell me why. …Speak, small Keeper. The cold is patient, but I am older than patience."*

**Garrick the Last Hewer** — a mountain quarryman, gruff and weathered, the last of a crew that once cut crystal from the high seams. He stayed when the others left. Knows every path and ledge on the ridge and grieves, in his flat way, the Keepers he's watched pass through and not return. Gives the player the lead on the previous Keeper.
> *"Aye, the old one came through. Said little. Touched the cold-iron vein, looked north a long while, and went on. I told them the path. I tell everyone the path. They go anyway."*

### What you claim
The **Stoneback crystal/cold-iron seam** — the richest node in the world, but the hardest to reach and hold (steep terrain slows everyone; the enemies here are fewer but tougher). The mid-game prize.

## 4. South — Mirewood · *"What the Water Keeps"*

### Premise
Drowned low country south of the valley. The main tide of Hollow Ones marches up from here, because the Withering pools in still water the way cold pools in a cellar. Beneath the black water lies a **sunken settlement — the first valley the Withering ever took**, its hall and homes still standing under the murk. The fog never fully lifts. The footing lies. Mirewood is the oppressive march, the one that feels like it's already lost, and the one that holds the oldest answers.

### Plotline — *What the Water Keeps*
The southern waves are getting worse, and they're carrying something — relics of the drowned hall, dragged up by the tide. The Keeper poles into the mire (relighting ward-stones on the few dry hummocks) toward the sunken settlement to find what the old Folk hid before they fell. What's down there is a record — the first account of the Wound, written by people who saw it open and chose to drown their own valley to slow it. The thread ties the deepest dread to the deepest hope: the first Folk did not run. They held, and their holding bought a thousand years. So can the Keeper's.

### NPCs

**Old Sedge** *(the Ferryman)* — a silent boatman who poles the black water and ferries the Keeper across the mire. He asks a small price each crossing — a crystal, a memory, a name said aloud — and it is never clear whether he is Folk, spirit, or something the mire kept and reshaped. Liminal and unsettling, but not hostile. The bible's "grief, not evil" rule, made into a person.
> *(He does not speak. He holds out one hand, palm up, and waits. When you pay, he poles you out. When you reach the far hummock, he is already gone.)*

**Vessa, the Lantern-Widow** — the last living soul of a hamlet at the mire's edge, keeping a single lantern lit in a half-flooded hall for folk who will not return. She has been waiting so long that waiting has become who she is. She holds the drowned hall's record and will give it to a Keeper who proves the war is still being fought.
> *"I keep the lamp because someone must. If they come back wet and cold and forgetting their own names, I want there to be a light they can walk toward. You think that's foolish. You keep a lamp too, Keeper. Yours is just bigger."*

### What you claim
The **Mirewood bog node** (peat, marsh-crystal, or a salvage-from-the-drowned-hall node) — under the heaviest enemy pressure in the world, since this is the main march. High risk, the node that tests whether your defenses can hold a forward position at all.

## 5. North — Corrupted Ashwood · *"The Failing Wards"*

### Premise
The dead forest. Ash-grey ground, trees that died standing, purple corruption fog drifting between the trunks. **This is where the Withering came from** — the closest the living realm reaches toward the Wound — and the Hollow Ones feel *native* here, like they belong to the rot rather than march through it. The first Keepers planted their strongest ward-stones along the north march to hold this line. They are failing now, one by one. To walk north is to walk toward the source, and to feel the song go thin around you.

### Plotline — *The Failing Wards*
The northern ward-stones are going dark in sequence, and each one that fails lets the Ashwood creep a little closer to the valley. The Keeper pushes north relighting them — the hardest exploration in the game, the song thinnest, the Keeper's own thoughts beginning to fray near the dead places (a soft sanity/forgetting mechanic: linger too long past a dark ward and the screen mutes, the HUD dims, the Heart's voice fades — relight the ward to come back to yourself). At the last lit ward stands **Old Bram**, the final north-warden, half-Withered and holding one stone alight by will alone. And at the edge of the lit ground waits a Hollow One that does not attack — **the One Who Remembers** — who stops, and tilts its head, and seems to know the Keeper's name. This march is the one that points at Alduin, and at the master who walked into the dark and did not return.

### NPCs

**Old Bram** — the last of the north-march wardens, who refused to leave his post when the rot took the forest. He is half-Withered himself now — grey at the edges, forgetting words mid-sentence — but lucid in flashes, and holding his last ward-stone lit by sheer stubbornness. Tragic and clear-eyed. He teaches the player the deepest ward-craft and is, gently, a warning of what the north does to those who stay.
> *"I keep one stone. Just the one. When my words go — and they go, more each day — I'll still know to keep the one stone lit. Some things you don't forget. You just… stop being able to say them."*

**The One Who Remembers** — a Hollow One that comes to the edge of a lit ward and *stops*. It does not raise a weapon. It tilts its head as if listening to the song it can no longer hear, and it seems — impossibly — to know the Keeper. It never speaks (the Hollow Ones' voices were the first thing the cold took). It is the bible's whole thesis in one figure: grief wearing armor, a person still faintly there under the rot. Whether it is connected to the previous Keeper or to Alduin is the kind of question the game should let the player wonder, not answer outright.
> *(It does not speak. When you light the ward, it stands a moment in the new warmth. Then it turns, and walks back into the fog, and does not look at you again. The Heart, very quietly, hums a note you have never heard it make.)*

### What you claim
The **Ashwood corrupt-crystal node** — the strangest and most valuable harvest, gem-sap turned by the Withering into something that is powerful and faintly *wrong*. Holding it means holding ground at the very edge of the rot. The endgame node, where Defend-and-Explore points toward the eventual march on the Wound itself.

## 8. Open questions for the owner

1. **Is the ward-tether the exploration mechanic?** It unifies Rung 3 + resource nodes + the Arcane Tower lore. If yes, it should become its own work order (suggested: WO-112, ward-stone relight + reach system) and feed WO-111.
2. **How explicit should the Alduin / previous-Keeper threads get?** My pick: keep them as implication in v1 (Wren's healers, Bram's fading, the One Who Remembers). Name nothing the player can't infer. It matches the bible's restraint.
3. **Do NPCs give repeatable side-objectives or one-time story beats?** My pick: one anchor questline per region for v1 (the four plotlines above), with the resource node as the repeatable layer underneath.
4. **The forgetting mechanic in the Ashwood** (HUD dims / song fades past a dark ward) — keep as a real mechanic, or flavor-only? My pick: a gentle real mechanic, fully reversible, never punishing — it sells the dread without frustrating.

Defaults if unanswered: ward-tether adopted, threads kept implicit, one questline per region, forgetting kept as a gentle reversible mechanic.
