## The myth (the one-page telling — v2, canon-true)

Through the Bright Centuries, Elarion was the city other cities told stories about. Its walls
were never built to keep anything out — they were built wide, to hold everyone in: smiths and
jewelers, farmers and fools, and at the center of it all the Heart of Elarion, the world-tree
whose light was the breath of all living things. The old rune for the Heart appears on rings
and city-mark work from that age. No one carved wards beside it. It never occurred to anyone
that the Heart might need protecting.

Then came the Dimming.

No army marched on Elarion. Something older than armies happened: a grief older than memory
crept into the world, and the Heart's light began to fail. And as the light dimmed everywhere
else first, the Hollow Ones rose — not monsters, but the broken: everything the failing light
let go of, drawn across the dark to the last warmth they could feel. They came the way cold
comes for a fire. They did not want gold. They wanted to stand near the light — and what the
Hollow reach for, they unmake.

The walls held. The walls were the only thing that held. Inside them, everything that could
burn, burned; everything that could break, broke; and the people of Elarion fell holding the
line around a tree whose light was going out.

But a light defended to the last does not go out. It goes in.

The Heart drew down to a single ember and slept. And the ones who died holding the line did not
entirely leave — the tree kept them, the way wood keeps a season. Ages passed. Grass swallowed
the streets. Moss took the anvils. Rain wore the names off the well-stones, and nature, patient
and unbothered, tucked the whole city into the ground like a body laid to rest. Travelers who
passed the walls called it a ruin. They were wrong. It was a seed.

Then one answered. A knight — Grom — sworn to carry a single ember back into the dark.

Stand at the Heart and you can feel what the ember remembers: where the forge stood, where the
mill turned, where the market laughed and haggled on summer evenings. As the light rises, that
memory walks — small spirits drifting out from the roots to gather wood and iron and grain,
working the land the way their hands once did. The old folk would have found it fitting. The
people of Elarion never could sit idle.

They are called the Echoes. The city is named for them now.

The walls still stand. The well still draws. Everything else is yours to raise — where you
choose, as you choose, knowing what the first Elarion learned too late: everything you build
can be reached, and everything that can be reached can burn. Build wisely. Drive back the dark
and the Heart grows; let the Heart grow and the Echoes grow stronger; and one lantern at a
time, the city other cities told stories about will tell its own story again.

The Hollow Ones are already coming. Warmth draws them still — and you are about to make this
the warmest place in the world.

Hold the last light.
