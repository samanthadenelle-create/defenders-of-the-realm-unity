# Storyline — Defenders of the Realm v2 (Elarion-of-the-Spire)

**Status:** v2 storyline. Reframes the world after the Heart-Tree, the Keep, and the wilderness ring were removed and the **Cathedral Spire** was raised at the centre of Elarion. Supersedes the Heart-Tree premise in `docs/narrative-bible.md` while keeping its tone, vocabulary, and characters intact (the Withering, the Hollow Ones, Alduin the Mournful, the master's letters, the cycle).

**Tone:** Heroic, escalating, marketing-clean. Warm at the edges, real stakes at the core. Short sentences. Old bones, modern English. Chibi wizard meets Gothic spire meets KayKit market stalls — pitched for a mobile RPG: every noun should slot into a banner, every beat into a screenshot.

**Continuity note:** The Heart-Tree is referenced in past tense. It was burned. The spire is what the Folk built after. Everything else in the bible — the Withering, the Hollow Ones, Alduin, the master, the cycle — still holds.

---

## 1. The central conflict, reframed

A hundred winters ago the Heart-Tree burned. Alduin the Mournful — once the realm's greatest healer, then its first mourner — walked too close to the Wound trying to mend it and came back wrong. He carried the Withering home in his lungs and breathed it onto the roots of Elarion. The Tree took three days to die. The Folk watched.

What they did next is why the village is still here.

The Folk had no king to call. The court had already fled south. So the village's stoneworkers, glassmakers, and the apprentice-Keepers who survived the burn raised a **spire of pale stone** over the Tree's stump and bound the last breath of its song inside it. The spire is not a church in the way the old realms had churches. It is an **instrument** — a needle of stone driven through the wound the Tree left, and when the wind moves through its bell-iron the spire **sings**. One long held note, low and steady, that the valley has heard every hour of every day for a hundred years.

The note is the realm's heartbeat now. While the spire sings, the valley stays green and the dark stays on the far side of the wall. But the spire is not a tree — it cannot grow, cannot heal itself, cannot answer the Withering with new leaf. It can only **hold the note**. And it has been holding for a hundred years.

The Hollow Ones come because they can hear the note fraying. The Withering comes because the spire is the last thing on this side of the Wound that still remembers the realm's music, and silence wants the rest of it. High over the city, every few nights, the dragon **Syndrath the Devourer** passes at altitude, head tilted, listening — counting the spire's beats before he comes for them.

**The conflict in one sentence:** _The Heart-Tree is gone, the spire holds the last of its song, and every wave of Hollow Ones is the dark testing whether the stone will hold the chord as long as the wood did._

## 2. Why Elarion has no king, but does have a cathedral

There was a Keep once. There was a banner. The night the Tree burned, the king's household rode south with the relics they could carry, leaving the Folk to bury what they could not. The Folk did not chase them. They never came back.

In the years after, the **Choristers** — the apprentice-Keepers who had been too young to walk to the Wound with their masters — took up what no king would. They are not priests. They are not nobles. They are the ones who stand at the spire's foot and **hold the chord** when the wind drops, humming the part of the song the stone cannot make on its own. The eldest Chorister of each generation is called simply **the Keeper**, by old habit. There are usually two or three of them living. The current Keeper is young. Her master walked to the Wound four winters back, the same way Alduin once did, and did not return. The chord is hers to hold now.

There is no court. There is no crown. The spire is the only authority the Folk recognise — and the spire's only authority is _it is still singing._ This is enough. This is the deal Elarion makes with itself: no one rules, but someone holds the chord, and the chord holds everyone.

## 3. The wave-defence threat — the Withering's Choir

The Hollow Ones still come. The new name the Folk have for the marches is **the Choir** — because they sing too, faintly, in a counter-key that drags against the spire's note, and every voice they add tries to pull the chord flat. The Choir is led, as before, by **Alduin the Mournful**, who reforms wherever the Withering finds purchase. He is no longer one man. He is composite — every Keeper and healer the Wound drank, wearing the same face, conducting the same dirge.

The Choir comes in three voices the village has learned to fear:

- **The Frost-Voice** — Hollow Ones who climbed down from the dead northern peaks. Slow, patient, hard to break. They come for the spire's foundation, to crack the resonance at the root.
- **The Ember-Voice** — what is left of the southern wanderers Alduin caught on the road. They burn as they march. They come for the spire's bell-iron, to melt it dumb.
- **The Half-Voice** — wisps, almost-not-there. The Withering's newest work. They drift through the wall like the wall is suggestion. They come for the spire's violet glass, to shatter the upper register.

The **Arcane Tower** — the one defensive structure the Folk still know how to build — is the only thing whose stone the Choir cannot pass. The Folk forgot how to raise Keeps. They forgot how to forge banners. They remember how to raise a tower with a resonance-stone in its crown, because the spire taught them, and because a tower is a small spire, and a small spire still sings.

(The wall around the village is old and cracked and can be repaired — `BuildMenu` has _Repair Wall_ and _Build Tower_ for exactly this reason. There is nothing else to build, because nothing else holds the chord.)

## 4. The three heroes — why they are at Elarion now

### The Wizard — _the Keeper_ (default class)

A chibi-small girl in a starry purple robe, the youngest of three Choristers still living. She inherited the chord at sixteen when her master walked north. She has not been ready any day since, and the spire has not minded. The crystal staff she carries is a splinter of the old Tree, set in silver — the one piece of the Heart anyone managed to save the night it burned, and it still hums in tune with the spire. She is the player's default because she is the closest to the song; she hears the chord the way other people hear weather. **She is at Elarion because she has nowhere else to be — the Heart chose her, and the choice was final.**

### The Knight — _Sir Bram of the Last Banner_

The household that rode south the night the Tree burned had one knight who turned his horse around at the second crossroads and rode back. He was twelve. He buried what he could. He stayed. He is now older, armoured in plate the Folk's smith made from melted-down banner-fittings, and he is the closest thing the village has to a soldier. He does not believe the spire will hold. He believes _he_ will hold, and the spire is what he is holding for. **He is at Elarion because he made a promise to a Tree that was already dead by the time he made it, and he has not broken a promise yet.**

### The Ranger — _Nessa of the Outer Paths_

A trapper and pathfinder out of the wilderness north of the valley. She has been the one finding the Hollow Ones on the long roads for ten years, marking them with bone-tags so the Choristers know where the Withering is creeping. She does not live in the village. She comes when the spire's note wavers — which it has, three times this season — and she has stayed longer each time. **She is at Elarion because the chord keeps slipping, and she has worked out that this means the Choir is close, and someone has to walk the edges of the wall when the Keeper cannot.**

(All three heroes pull from the same well — the Heart's last song, carried by the spire — which is why even the Knight and the Ranger can spend mana on ward-resonance. The spire does not care who holds the chord. It only cares that someone does.)

## 5. The three pets — beings of the new world

### Twilight Sprite — _the Bell-Child_

A small violet fairy, born when the Heart-Tree was burning and the last of its crystal shattered. She is not a daughter of the spire — she is a daughter of the **moment between the Tree and the spire**, the breath the realm took before it decided to keep going. She lives in the spire's bell-iron and only comes out when a Chorister walks past humming the chord. She has bonded to the current Keeper. The other Choristers are politely jealous.

### Flame Pup — _the Hearth-Heir_

The Folk's hearth-fires never went out the year the Tree burned. The Pup is the spirit of those hearths braided together — the small fierce thing that survives in a kettle when nothing else will. She sleeps on warm stones in the Pet House. She bites the cold when it comes too close to the door. She is older than her eyes suggest, and she remembers the Tree, though she will not say what it sounded like.

### Ice Wolf — _the Cold-Wandered_

A frost-spirit from the dead northern peaks. He came down the mountain four winters back — the same night the current Keeper's master walked north. The village has decided not to ask whether these things are connected. He pads silent through the snow at the village's edge. He bares his teeth at the Frost-Voice when it comes. He does not look away.

(All three pets draw their power from the spire's song the same way the heroes do. A pet placed at a building tends it. A pet placed in a defensive slot fights for it. They are not owned. They are kept, the way the chord is kept — by being held, by being attended to.)

## 6. The dungeons, as story beats

### Healer's Cottage (west portal)

A small cottage in the wilderness west of the wall — the home of a Chorister-trained herbalist who used to ride out from Elarion to mend the Folk in the further hamlets. She has not come home in three weeks. The Keeper goes to find her.

The Keeper finds her cottage standing. Kettle still warm. Garden still kept. No body. What the Keeper does find, in the upstairs desk, is an **apothecary's journal** — and it is in handwriting she has seen before, in the spire's old archives. The cottage's herbalist was Alduin's apprentice, two hundred years ago, and the cottage is where his _kindness_ still lives, in the slow water and the thyme and the lullaby he taught her to sing for a child with a cough. (The questline _The Healer's Garden_ in `docs/dungeons-storyline.md` still applies here verbatim — the cottage is the first place the Keeper learns Alduin was once a healer.)

**The story beat:** _The dark was kind, once. The dark had a name. The dark still has a garden, and the garden is still growing._

### Folk's Old Granary (east portal)

The granary on the village's east side was abandoned twelve winters ago when the Folk consolidated their stores closer to the spire. It is supposed to be empty. It isn't.

When the Keeper walks in, the floor is laid with **oil-soaked rope coils** — long, deliberate, the kind a person sets when they mean to burn something carefully, not by accident. The silos are still half-full of barrels. The grain piles in the back have been _moved_ — pulled aside to expose a **cellar hatch** that the Folk had forgotten was there, ringed now with three lit candles. Someone is in the cellar. Someone has been there for weeks. They are **storing oil and waiting for a frost-wave** — the granary is east-facing, the spire is downwind of it, and a fire here on the right night would carry to the spire's bell-iron and unsing it.

The Keeper does not find a cult. She finds a single old man at the workbench, who turns and is not surprised to see her. He is one of the southern wanderers Alduin caught on the road — Ember-Voice, half-Hollow, still mostly himself, still remembering enough to be ashamed of what he is about to do. He stops. He tells her where the rest of his Choir is hiding. The granary does not burn.

**The story beat:** _Some of them remember. Some of them can be talked to. Most of them cannot. The Keeper has to learn which is which before the wave hits._

(This slots cleanly into the existing _The Folk Who Forgot_ questline as its opening beat — see `docs/dungeons-storyline.md` §4.2. The "Ambient NPC" on the granary floor is this old man. His line is in §7 below.)

## 9. The Dragon's Shadow

Every few nights, with no warning the Folk can predict, **Syndrath the Devourer** passes high over Elarion. He does not stoop. He does not land. He does not breathe fire. He flies a long slow arc from the dead northern peaks to the southern horizon, head tilted, **counting the spire's beats** — measuring how flat the chord has gone since the last time he listened. The villagers stop in the street when his shadow crosses the market. The hearth-dogs go still. The spire's note shivers — just a quarter-tone, just for a breath — and then steadies. He vanishes back into the cloud. He has been doing this for a hundred winters. He is the only Hollow thing the Withering did not make, and the only one the Folk cannot fight: he is the apex wave-boss, and these fly-bys are the timer he is keeping on himself. One night the chord will not steady when his shadow falls. That is the night he comes down.

In game terms: Syndrath spawns as an ambient cinematic actor at low frequency between waves and between acts, flies a high-altitude path across the play-space, and despawns at the far edge. He is non-interactive in this state — no damage, no targeting, no aggro. The shot is a hold-frame. The spire's audio loop should detune briefly during his pass and re-tune as he leaves. The dragon's silhouette is the threat clock the player cannot see numerically.
