# Suno Battle Music Prompts — Defenders of the Realm: Echoes of Elarion

**Goal:** Generate immersive, dynamic battle music for overworld combat in a 3D medieval fantasy village defense game.

---

## Prompt Philosophy

**What works for Suno:**
1. **Specific instruments** (not just "orchestral")
2. **Tempo + energy level** (BPM, "fast-paced," "driving")
3. **Mood + emotion** (epic, tense, victorious, desperate)
4. **Reference styles** (film composers, game OSTs)
5. **Structure** (build-up, climax, resolution)
6. **Immersion cues** (ambient layers, 3D soundscape, spatial)

**What doesn't work:**
- Vague ("nice music")
- Too many conflicting moods
- Overly long descriptions (Suno works better with focused briefs)
- Technical jargon (stick to feeling/reference)

---

## Primary Prompt: Overworld Battle (General Combat)

**Best for:** Wave combat in the village, medium intensity, persistent threat

```
Generate an immersive overworld battle theme for a medieval fantasy village 
under siege. The setting is a 3D open world with low-poly aesthetic charm. 
The music should feel heroic but grounded—protecting home, not conquering 
empires. 

Instrumentation: String section (driving, urgent), French horns (bold calls), 
war drums (steady 4/4 beat), muted brass (stabs, punctuation), light synth 
pad underneath (spatial, immersive 3D feel). No ethereal/magical elements—
this is visceral and real.

Tempo: 130–140 BPM, driving and relentless. The rhythm pushes forward; players 
feel the pressure to act.

Mood: Tense but hopeful. Dangerous but not hopeless. Similar to Kingdom Come: 
Deliverance battle themes or Baldur's Gate 3 combat—medieval authenticity 
mixed with adventure energy.

Structure: 
- Intro (8 bars): Drum and horn call, establishing urgency
- Verse 1 (16 bars): Strings join, tension builds
- Chorus (16 bars): Full orchestration, climax—moment of combat intensity
- Verse 2 (16 bars): Slight drop, but momentum maintained (enemies keep coming)
- Final Chorus (16 bars): Bigger, more heroic—player turning the tide

Duration: ~2 minutes (fits wave combat rhythm)

Key: Minor key (D minor or E minor) for drama, but bright strings keep it 
from feeling hopeless.

Special detail: Add subtle ambient 3D layers—distant enemy sounds, wind through 
trees, stone clattering. Make it feel like you're *in* the village, not watching 
from afar.
```

## Variation 1: Intense Wave (High Pressure Moment)

**Best for:** Late-game waves, boss enemies, desperate defense

```
Generate an intense, desperate battle theme for a medieval fantasy village 
as the final wave closes in. The setting is a 3D open-world battlefield. 
This is the moment when everything is on the line.

Instrumentation: Full orchestra—violent strings (tremolo, sawing), loud French 
horns (panic/rallying cry), heavy war drums (aggressive double-bass kick), 
blaring brass (stabs), low strings pizzicato (heartbeat effect). Add distant 
screams or animal roars (very subtle, 3D spatial).

Tempo: 145–160 BPM, fast and relentless. No time to breathe.

Mood: Desperate, adrenaline-fueled, but still defiant. Not dark or hopeless—
players can still win, but they *must* act *now*. Similar to Dark Souls boss 
themes or the final phase of Hollow Knight—intense but not cinematic.

Structure:
- Intro (4 bars): Drum assault, no melody—pure rhythm and tension
- Verse 1 (12 bars): Strings and horns clash, building panic
- Climax (20 bars): Everything at once—full orchestra, relentless drive
- Drop (4 bars): Brief moment of... is this the end?
- Final Push (16 bars): Heroic comeback—strings surge, horns triumph

Duration: ~2 minutes

Key: E minor or F minor (dark, urgent)

Special detail: Build in a subtle *recovery* moment halfway through—players 
need to feel they can turn things around, even when desperate.
```

## Variation 2: Victory / Post-Battle Transition

**Best for:** Moment enemies are defeated, transitioning back to building phase

```
Generate a triumphant yet grounded victory theme for a medieval fantasy village 
that just repelled a wave of enemies. The setting is a 3D open world. The mood 
is celebratory but earned—heroes are tired, buildings are damaged, but they won.

Instrumentation: Full orchestra with emphasis on bright strings (major key), 
trumpet fanfare (victory call, but not over-dramatic), timpani (ceremonial beats), 
light choir or vocal hum (optional—people celebrating). Minimal drums—the pace 
slows after the battle.

Tempo: 100–110 BPM, slower and more stately than battle. Players have time to 
breathe.

Mood: Heroic, proud, weary but satisfied. Like standing in a damaged village 
watching the sun rise after a long night. Similar to Zelda: Breath of the Wild's 
post-boss themes—epic but warm.

Structure:
- Intro (8 bars): Trumpet fanfare (brief, bright)
- Verse 1 (16 bars): Strings swell, celebrating survival
- Chorus (16 bars): Full orchestra, moment of triumph
- Bridge (8 bars): Reflective—acknowledging the cost
- Outro (12 bars): Gentle resolution, preparing for next phase

Duration: 1–1.5 minutes

Key: D major or G major (bright, major key—opposite of battle)

Special detail: Include ambient village sounds (birds, wind, distant voices). 
Make it feel like the world is *alive* again after the chaos.
```

## Variation 3: Boss/Arena Battle (High Stakes)

**Best for:** WO-369 Arena Monument, special boss encounters, endgame battles

```
Generate an epic, climactic boss battle theme for a medieval fantasy arena 
monument. The setting is a sacred stone monument in a 3D open world—this is 
THE battle that matters. Grand, mythic, but grounded in medieval reality 
(not power-metal fantasy).

Instrumentation: Full symphony—tremolo strings (intense, driven), powerful French 
horns (regal and commanding), war drums and timpani (thunderous), low brass 
(ominous undertone), occasional synth pad for immersion/3D depth. Minimal vocals—
maybe a wordless choral swell at climax. No electric elements.

Tempo: 135–150 BPM, powerful and inexorable. The boss doesn't hurry, but it's 
relentless.

Mood: Mythic confrontation. This feels *important*—the fate of Elarion hangs 
in balance. Epic but not cheesy. Similar to Shadow of the Colossus, or Bloodborne 
boss themes—grand but grounded.

Structure:
- Intro (12 bars): Ominous silence broken by single horn call
- Verse 1 (20 bars): Strings and drums build—the enemy approaches
- Climax 1 (24 bars): Full orchestra—initial clash, player fighting back
- Bridge (8 bars): Moment of doubt—is this even winnable?
- Verse 2 (20 bars): Player rallies—horns become heroic instead of menacing
- Final Climax (28 bars): Everything peaks—the ultimate confrontation
- Resolution (8 bars): Victory or doom—the music doesn't decide, player does

Duration: 3–4 minutes (longer for endurance battles)

Key: E minor or C minor (dark, powerful), shifting to major key at final victory 
moment (player's agency)

Special detail: Add spatial ambient elements—wind around the monument, distant 
cheers from villagers watching, the weight of stone. Make players feel the 
sacred ground beneath their feet.
```

## Variation 4: Exploration with Danger (Ambient Battle)

**Best for:** When enemies are nearby but not yet fighting (heightened alert state)

```
Generate an ambient but tense exploration theme for a medieval fantasy world 
with lurking danger. The setting is a 3D open world—players are exploring, 
but something is *wrong*. Enemies are close. Not active battle yet, but 
imminent threat.

Instrumentation: Sparse strings (single violin, cello pizzicato), soft French 
horn in distance (ominous), minimal drums (heartbeat-like, 1 beat per 2 seconds), 
ambient pad (immersive 3D spatial layer), occasional wood creaks or stone shifts 
(environmental sounds). Very minimal orchestration.

Tempo: 90–100 BPM, slow and deliberate. The pace is walking speed, but tense.

Mood: Unease, dread, but not panic. Players should feel watched, threatened, 
but still in control. Similar to Zelda's "dungeon nearby" music or Hollow Knight's 
"Kingdom's Edge" ambient theme—something is wrong, but exploration continues.

Structure:
- Intro (16 bars): Sparse, letting silence breathe
- Verse 1 (24 bars): Single instrument, building unease
- Build (12 bars): Tempo subtly increases, pressure mounting
- Plateau (24 bars): Maintains tension—waiting, anticipating
- Outro (8 bars): Resolves into... action (battle music) or relief (threat 
  averted)

Duration: 2–3 minutes (loops during exploration)

Key: Minor key (D minor or A minor), with occasional chromatic dissonance to 
create unease

Special detail: Heavy use of 3D spatial audio cues—distant animal sounds, wind, 
stone. Make the *world* feel alive and menacing, not just the music.
```

## Tips for Best Results with Suno

### 1. **Reference Artists**
If Suno asks for style/artist reference, suggest:
- **Composers:** Austin Wintory (Journey), Jen Pogue (Baldur's Gate 3), Michiru Oshima (Kingdom Come: Deliverance)
- **Games:** Dark Souls, Hollow Knight, Kingdom Come: Deliverance, Zelda: Breath of the Wild, Mount & Blade II: Bannerlord
- **Avoid:** Hans Zimmer (too cinematic), John Williams (too orchestral-grand), video game metal (doesn't fit low-poly charm)

### 2. **Regenerate if:**
- Music feels too "cinematic" or film-like (you want game-immersive, not movie-epic)
- Synths sound too modern (ask for "no synths, pure medieval instruments")
- Pace feels wrong (re-specify BPM or "faster" / "slower")
- Instruments are off (specify exactly what you want: "strings, horns, drums—no choir")

### 3. **Iterate**
- Start with Primary Prompt
- If it's close but not quite right, iterate with small tweaks
- "Make it 10 BPM faster" or "Less dramatic, more grounded" are good refinements

### 4. **Chain Variations**
Generate all 4 variations (General, Intense, Victory, Boss) — then edit/splice them in Unity for context-aware transitions:
- Exploration → Enemies nearby? → Intense Wave → Victory
- Each variation is its own song, but they flow together

## Implementation in Unity

Once you have audio files from Suno:

**Setup:**
```csharp
// In BattleMusic.cs or similar
public enum BattleState { Exploration, Alert, Combat, Intense, Victory, Boss }

public void TransitionTo(BattleState state)
{
    switch (state)
    {
        case BattleState.Exploration:
            CrossFadeTo(_explorationAmbient, 2f);
            break;
        case BattleState.Alert:
            CrossFadeTo(_alertTheme, 1.5f);
            break;
        case BattleState.Combat:
            CrossFadeTo(_battleTheme, 1f);  // Sharp transition
            break;
        case BattleState.Intense:
            CrossFadeTo(_intenseWaveTheme, 0.5f);  // Abrupt (felt like escalation)
            break;
        case BattleState.Victory:
            CrossFadeTo(_victoryTheme, 2f);  // Gradual, celebratory
            break;
        case BattleState.Boss:
            CrossFadeTo(_bossTheme, 3f);  // Slow, mythic build-up
            break;
    }
}
```

**Trigger Points:**
- `WaveManager.OnWaveStart()` → Combat
- `WaveManager.BossWave()` → Boss
- `EnemyCount > 3 && AlertState` → Intense
- `WaveManager.OnWaveVictory()` → Victory
- `WaveManager.OnWaveEnd()` → Exploration

- [ ] Immersion cues included (3D spatial, ambient layers)
- [ ] Structure is clear (intro, build, climax, resolution)
