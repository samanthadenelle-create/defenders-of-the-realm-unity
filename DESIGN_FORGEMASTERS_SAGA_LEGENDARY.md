# Design — The Forgemasters' Saga (Windmill · Lumbermill · Armorer · Forge → Legendary Gear)

**Status:** DESIGN / deep narrative (creative call — owner approves names + final text).
**Builds on:** `DESIGN_VENDOR_STORYLINES_AND_QUESTLINES.md` (this is the *deep* version of the four
production crafters and the legendary tier they unlock). Feeds WO-106 gear/shop, WO-109/116 dialogue,
WO-151 progression, and the Spire/Necromancer finale.
**Crafting tiers:** Common → Fine → Master → **Legendary**. Only the four crafters *united* can reach Legendary.

---

## 0. The legend beneath Elarion — "The Aegis and the Sin"

Three generations ago, before the Dimming, Elarion's four crafts were **one workshop** under a single
master: **Aldric Emberhand**, the First Forgemaster. With the Heart at its brightest, Aldric forged the
**Aegis of Elarion** — a legendary set of arms and armor that drank the Tree's aether and made its bearer
unbreakable. Elarion never lost a siege while the Aegis stood the wall.

But aether is the Heart's lifeblood, and the Aegis drank deep. Some say Aldric's masterwork is the very
reason the Heart began, century by century, to **dim** — that the realm has been slowly bleeding into a
suit of armor ever since. When the champion who wore the Aegis fell anyway — betrayed, the armor cracked,
the blade snapped — the workshop **shattered into four** along the seams of that grief and blame:

- the **Forge** kept the steel and the guilt,
- the **Armory** kept the plate and the grudge,
- the **Lumbermill** kept the secret of Heartwood and the refusal to cut it,
- the **Windmill** kept the quench-oil and the peace nobody wanted.

The four crafters today are Aldric's heirs and apprentices' heirs. They have not shared a fire in thirty
years. The broken Aegis lies in pieces, scattered. And now a **Necromancer** in the Ashen Wastes drinks
what aether the Heart has left — and the only thing that has ever stopped an unbreakable enemy is an
unbreakable weapon. To beat him, the player must do the unthinkable: **reunite the craft and reforge a
Legendary** — knowing it may cost the Tree.

> This is the emotional engine: **legendary power has a price, and the people who can make it are the
> people most afraid to.** Every crafting upgrade is also a reconciliation.

## 1. The four crafters — deep characterization (want · wound · secret)

### Borin Emberhand — the Forge (NW)
- **Want:** to prove the Emberhand name still means *protect*, not *ruin*.
- **Wound:** he is Aldric's grandson. He grew up blamed for a Dimming that began before he was born. He
  keeps the **snapped hilt of the legendary blade** on his anvil and has never had the courage to reforge it.
- **Secret:** he knows the lost smithing technique — **the Threefold Fold** — but believes using it again
  is what doomed Elarion. He'd rather make a thousand honest swords than one great one.
- **Voice:** gruff, self-punishing, dry. Softens around anyone who treats his craft as honest work, not legend.

### Dame Halvard — the Armory (NW)
- **Want:** to make armor that *never fails again*.
- **Wound:** the champion who fell in the Aegis was her brother (or her oath-sworn — owner's call). She
  believes the Emberhand steel failed him; she has hated the forge ever since. She keeps his **dented
  breastplate** and will not let it be melted down.
- **Secret:** she alone remembers the **Oathweld** — the plating method that bonds armor to its bearer's
  will. She withholds it out of fear it will be wasted on another doomed hero.
- **Voice:** iron-spined, formal, grief under the discipline. Tests the player's resolve before her warmth.

### Old Pell — the Lumbermill (NW)
- **Want:** for the forest — and the Tree — to be left to heal.
- **Wound:** he was the woodward who cut the **Heartwood bough** for Aldric's Aegis. He has heard the Tree
  "weep" in the wind ever since and swore never to cut it again. He carries that single guilt heavier than all.
- **Secret:** only Heartwood — wood from a *living* bough of the Heart, **given, not taken** — can hold
  aether without burning. The legendary craft is impossible without it, and he is the only one who knows
  how to ask the Tree rather than wound it.
- **Voice:** slow, gentle, haunted. The moral conscience of the four.

### Mother Wren — the Windmill (NW / edge of Housing)
- **Want:** to see the four share a fire again before she's gone.
- **Wound:** her mill burned the night the Aegis fell — sparks from the forge, some say. She rebuilt it
  with her own hands and never spoke a word of blame, which is its own kind of accusation.
- **Secret:** the windmill grinds more than grain. Its great stones press the **Last Pressing** — an
  aether-binding **quench-oil** from heart-grain and tree-sap that lets steel *drink* without shattering.
  No quench, no Legendary. She's the keystone everyone forgot.
- **Voice:** warm, plain-spoken, quietly unbreakable. The peacemaker — and the one who finally says *yes*.

## 2. The saga — four acts (gated to village progression + region clears)

The player doesn't get Legendary by grinding. They earn it by **healing a thirty-year wound**, one act at
a time. Each act advances the crafting tier *and* the relationships.

### ACT I — "Honest Steel" (unlock the crafters; reach **Fine** tier)
Each crafter's personal questline (see the vendor doc: The Last Ember, Shields of the Fallen, Roots Run
Deep, Full Bellies/Full Ranks) plays out independently. The player relights the forge, recovers salvage,
clears Pell's grove, turns Wren's mill. By the end of Act I all four shops sell **Fine** gear and the
player has, without realizing it, gathered the four people who once were one workshop.
- **Beat:** in each Stage-END Talk, the crafter mentions *one of the others* with old bitterness — seeding the feud.

### ACT II — "The Old Fire" (the feud; reach **Master** tier)
Brom's rumor board surfaces it: to craft **Master** gear, the player needs two crafts at once (e.g.
Borin's steel quenched in Wren's oil; Halvard's plate cored with Pell's wood). But the crafters **refuse
to work together.** The player must broker peace, one pairing at a time:
1. **Borin ↔ Halvard** — recover her brother's truth: the Aegis didn't fail because the steel was weak;
   it failed because it was **incomplete** — never quenched, never warded (the workshop had already
   split). This absolves Borin's steel and reframes the grief.
2. **Pell ↔ the Forge** — Pell will not cut Heartwood for a weapon. The player proves the weapon is for
   *defense of the Tree itself*, not glory.
3. **Wren** — quietly agrees the moment the others stop fighting; her quest is simply to **get them all to
   the same hearth** for one night. (A warm, low-combat chapter — a meal, not a battle.)
- **Output:** Master tier unlocked; the four agree to *consider* the unthinkable.

### ACT III — "What Was Lost" (recover the techniques + components; gather across the regions)
The lost legendary craft is scattered as **four techniques/components**, each guarded in a region — so the
finale of this saga *is* the world tour:
1. **The Threefold Fold** (Forge) — Borin's missing folding pattern, lost with a journeyman in the
   **Stone Mountains** (W). Recover it from a Stonebelly stronghold.
2. **The Oathweld** (Armory) — Halvard remembers it but needs the **dented breastplate** restored, which
   means recovering the rest of her brother's company's gear from the **Frost Peaks** (N).
3. **The Heartwood Bough** (Lumbermill) — Pell must *ask* the Heart for a living bough. The rite requires
   cleansing the blight in the **Verdant Forest** (E) so the Tree is strong enough to give freely.
4. **The Last Pressing** (Windmill) — Wren's quench-oil needs **pure aether sap**, drawn from a node the
   Necromancer hasn't yet siphoned in the **Ashen Wastes** (S) — the most dangerous gather in the game.
- **Beat:** each component is also a **Keystone** for the Steward's master quest — the sagas converge.

### ACT IV — "The Reforging" (the Legendary craft; the choice; the finale)
All four crafters, all four components, one fire. The Aegis can be reborn. But the **quench demands a
heart of aether**, and the Heart is nearly dark. **The choice:**
- **Draw from the Heart** — the reforge is immediate and the Legendary is at full power, but the Tree dims
  further (harder finale, somber ending, NG+ "the long healing").
- **Gather from the cleansed regions** — slower (a final gather/defense across the four cleansed nodes),
  but the Tree is spared (the "true" ending where Elarion is rebuilt *and* whole).
Either way, the four crafters work as **one workshop** for the first time in thirty years — the
emotional payoff — and forge the **Legendary set** the player carries into the **Spire Defense** and the
**Necromancer** confrontation.

## 3. The Legendary payoff — the Aegis of Elarion (set + per-class arms)

Legendary requires **one component from each crafter** + the catalyst (aether of the player's choosing):

| Component | From | Craft restored |
|---|---|---|
| Reforged steel (the Threefold Fold) | Borin / Forge | the blade/head core |
| Oathweld plating | Halvard / Armory | the armor that binds to the bearer |
| Heartwood haft/limb/core | Old Pell / Lumbermill | the only material that holds aether |
| The Last Pressing (quench-oil) | Mother Wren / Windmill | the bond that lets steel drink aether |
| Aether catalyst | Heart **or** cleansed nodes (the choice) | the spark of life |

**Legendary set — the Aegis of Elarion** (shared armor + a class weapon each):
- **Aegis Plate / Garb** (armor, all classes): scales to class; "Oathweld" — a portion of damage taken is
  refunded as a ward that protects the *Heart* (mechanically ties your survival to the win condition).
- **Grom (Knight):** *Emberbrand, the Rekindled* — the reforged legendary greatblade; combo finisher
  releases stored aether as a shock.
- **Sylas (Archer):** *Heartwood Longbow* — Pell's living-wood limbs; charged shots pierce and "remember"
  the last struck foe.
- **Thrain (Mage):** *Aetherstaff* — quench-bonded crystal head; spells cost less the closer you fight
  (rewards the up-close DEF-269 combat).
- **Elara (Cleric):** *The Hallowed Censer* — heals also seed a small ward on the Heart/structures.

Each Legendary carries a **line of saga flavor text** naming the four crafters — so the player reads, in
their own gear, the story of the people who set aside thirty years of grief to make it.

## 4. Why the four crafts are *mechanically* interdependent (so the story has teeth)

- **Forge alone** = Common/Fine weapons (steel, no aether).
- **Forge + Windmill** = Master weapons (steel + quench bonds a *little* aether).
- **+ Lumbermill (Heartwood)** = aether-stable hafts → high Master / Legendary-ready.
- **+ Armorer (Oathweld)** = the set effect (ward) — armor + weapon as a *bonded* whole.
- **All four + catalyst** = **Legendary.**
This maps cleanly onto the crafting/upgrade system (WO-106/151): each tier literally requires another
crafter's output as an ingredient, so progression *forces* the reconciliation story.

## 5. Implementation hooks

- **Dialogue:** deep, stage-aware Yarn per crafter (`NPC_Forge.yarn`, `NPC_Armorer.yarn`,
  `NPC_Lumbermill.yarn`, `NPC_Windmill.yarn`) with Act-gated branches; reconciliation scenes are
  two-NPC Yarn nodes (Brom's hearth as the staging room).
- **Components/Keystones:** data entries (`Assets/Data/Canonical/`) flagged `legendaryComponent`; the
  reforge recipe in `CraftingRecipeCatalog` requires all four + a catalyst id.
- **Crafting tiers:** extend `GearCatalog`/`GearLoadout` with a `Tier` enum (Common/Fine/Master/Legendary)
  and ingredient gating; Legendary recipe locked until `QuestService` says Act IV complete.
- **The choice:** a Yarn `<<command: ReforgeChoice heart|regions>>` setting a persisted flag that affects
  finale difficulty + ending + an NG+ seed.
- **Persistence:** saga stage, components, choice → GameState, wallet-keyed (persistence lane).
- **Suggested WOs:** (a) crafting-tier + legendary-recipe system; (b) the 4 deep Yarn files + 3
  reconciliation scenes; (c) Legendary set items + the Aegis ward effect; (d) reforge choice → finale wiring.
- **Tone guardrail:** keep it bittersweet, not grimdark — Elarion is hopeful. The saga is about *mending*,
  and the warmest beat in the game (Act II's shared meal) should land harder than any boss.
